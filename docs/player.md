# Player guide

How the GBF casual competition works, and where to see your games. Live site: https://gbf-competition.vercel.app

You do not need an account. Everything players need is public and view-only. Only the scorekeeper signs in.

## Where to look

| Page | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Schedule (`/schedule`) | Every match by round: court, time, players and live or final scores |
| Standings (`/standings`) | Rankings for all players |
| Your player page (`/players/<id>`) | Your own games, partners and results (tap your nickname) |
| Past events (`/events`) | Results from finished competitions |

Use the language toggle in the header to switch between English and 繁體中文.
Players are shown by **nickname**, so check yours is spelled the way you want with the scorekeeper before play starts.

## Format

- Players whose nickname ends in **-F** are the Philippines team. Every match is **Philippines against the rest**.
- Both sides are the same type: men vs men, women vs women, or mixed vs mixed. Women may also play two men with a head start.
- All four players in a match are within one grade of each other (same grade where possible).
- Everyone plays **at most 6 games**. Nearly everyone gets all 6.
- You will not be in two matches in the same round, and partners are not repeated where it can be avoided.
- Matches are **time-limited** (for example 15 minutes), so a draw is possible.

Match panels are colour-coded: blue is men's doubles, pink is mixed doubles, lilac is women's doubles and coral is handicap (women against men, with a head start for the women).

## Scoring

| Result | Points |
|---|---|
| Win | 2 |
| Draw | 1 |
| Loss | 0 |

Ties in the standings are broken by:

1. Points difference
2. Points scored

Players still level after that share a rank, shown as `T-n` (for example T-3).

## On the day

1. Find your next match on the Schedule page. Open rounds are listed first.
2. Go to your court when your round is called.
3. A match marked **Live** is in play. When the scorekeeper saves the result it becomes **Final**.
4. If a score looks wrong, tell the scorekeeper. Only they can correct it.
