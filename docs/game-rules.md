# Game rules

Rules for GBF casual competitions as the app runs them. Items marked **To confirm** are not set by the app, so the organisers decide them before the event.

## Format

- Doubles only: **men's doubles** first, then **mixed doubles**.
- Every man plays the same number of men's doubles games (default 4).
- Every woman plays the same number of mixed doubles games (default 4). Women play mixed only.
- Men fill the other two places in each mixed match as **extra games**. The draw always picks the men with the fewest games, so extras are spread evenly (usually within 2 games of each other).
- Several matches run at the same time, one per court. Each round fills the lower court numbers with men's doubles first, then mixed.

## The draw

- No player is in two matches in the same round, and no court is used twice in a round.
- Partners are not repeated where it can be avoided.
- Players who just sat out are preferred for the next round.
- Optional grades are used to keep the two sides balanced. A is strongest, then B, C, D and so on up to I.
- **Partner pairing rules:**
  - A D-grade player is never partnered with a B-grade player.
  - Some players (for example a C-grade player) are happy to partner D-grade players. Tick **Happy to partner D grade** for them on the Players tab, and the draw prefers them as partners for D-grade players.
  - The draw applies the B-with-D rule on its own. If you edit a match by hand and break it, the Schedule tab shows an amber warning.
- Half of each woman's mixed games are **handicap** games (two women against two men, with a head start). The number is set on the Schedule tab (default 2 of her 4 mixed games). The draw picks the men so the head start rules below apply.
- Organisers can edit any match (players, court, round), add matches by hand, or regenerate the draw until play starts.
- A draw that would run past the event's time limit is refused.

## Match length

- Every match is **time-limited** (set per event, for example 15 minutes). When time is up the score on the board is the result, so draws are possible.
- **To confirm:** points per game or points cap, if any, and who calls time when a match is still running.

## Scoring

| Result | Points |
|---|---|
| Win | 2 |
| Draw | 1 |
| Loss | 0 |

Standings are ranked by:

1. Competition points
2. Points difference (points scored minus points conceded)
3. Points scored

Players still level after that share a rank, shown as `T-n` (for example T-3).

## Head start (handicap)

A head start evens up a match when one side is made up of women and the other of men. The women's side starts the game with extra points on the board; the men's side starts at 0.

**When it applies**

- Two women against two men: full head start.
- One woman plus one man against two men: half the full head start.
- Any other line-up (for example mixed pairs on both sides, or all men): no head start.

**How many points (two women against two men)**

| Women's grade compared with the men's | Head start |
|---|---|
| Same grade (for example C women vs C men) | 4 points |
| One grade lower (for example D women vs C men) | 6 points |

- Each side's grade is the average of its players' grades (A = 1, B = 2, C = 3, D = 4), rounded to the nearest whole grade.
- One woman plus one man against two men gets half: 2 points at the same grade, 3 points at one grade lower.

**How it counts**

- Head start points are part of the score. They count in the result, points scored and points difference like any other points.
- The app shows the head start on the match (for example "Women start +4") on the schedule and score-entry screens, with its own coral colour. It does not add the points itself: the scorekeeper includes them when entering the score.
- In the draw, handicap matches are stored as mixed doubles and appear on screen as **Handicap doubles** whenever two women (or a woman and a man) play against two men.

**To confirm:** the head start when the women's side is a grade or more *stronger* than the men's, or two or more grades lower, and how to round when the two grades average exactly halfway.

## Results and disputes

- Only the scorekeeper (the admin) enters scores. A match is **Live** once started and **Final** once the result is saved.
- A saved result can be reset and re-entered by the scorekeeper if it was wrong.
- **To confirm:** how players raise a score query, and who has the final say on court (for example, the scorekeeper).

- Players use nicknames, which are shown publicly.
- Be on your court when your round is called, so the schedule stays on time.
